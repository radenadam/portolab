# Charts

These standards apply to every chart, whether it lives here or in the feature that owns its subject. [Decision 0029](../../docs/decisions/0029-the-server-draws-every-chart-as-svg.md) explains why the server draws charts.

- The server draws a chart in SVG as part of its page, so the chart shows before JavaScript loads. What a chart does in the browser, such as a crosshair, a tooltip, or a label placed again, enhances the drawing, and before JavaScript runs the page shows no control that waits for it.
- A chart places its marks, its texts, and the ends of its lines at fractions of its plot, as `PlotFraction`, because only the browser knows the width of the plot. A length that is not a position in the plot is a multiple of the size of the text of the chart, in em, so the chart keeps its proportions when a reader enlarges the text. The texts of the marks of an axis use the font of codes, DM Mono, which decision 0028 sets for them and whose characters are equally wide, so the room for such a text is counted in widths of a character. A name beside a point of a map uses the font of the text, as [decision 0035](../../docs/decisions/0035-the-map-names-its-points-in-words-and-keeps-the-name-of-the-reader.md) sets, so the server places it from an estimate of its width, and the browser places it again from the width that it measures.
- The reader's portfolio and every stock the reader holds keep their name at every width, ahead of every other point. Only their value may drop when room runs out. The server and the browser place these names before the labels of lines, the IHSG, and the stocks that the reader does not hold, which get names only where room remains. A kept name stays apart from other names and nearer its own point before it keeps clear of other marks, and it may cross its own mark and use the room beside the plot that the axis leaves for it, as decision 0035 sets.
- An axis marks values no closer than the smallest step that the format of its texts writes apart, which `niceTicks` takes from the format, such as `smallestPercentStep`, so no two marks read the same. Its tests include values that spread less than that step.
- A chart that a reader explores answers a pointer, a finger, and the keys of the pattern of ARIA that fits it, such as a slider for the dates of a chart over time. Its heading names it, a screen reader reads the marked point as its value, and Escape hides what the chart shows over its drawing.
- The line or the point that a chart is about, which is the reader's own or the one that the reader opened, takes the color of the reader's own point, and a line that it is compared with, such as the IHSG over time, takes muted ink, so a chart reads the same on every screen. On a map, the IHSG is a diamond in ink, as the references of decision 0028 draw it, because its shape already sets it apart from the dots of the stocks.
