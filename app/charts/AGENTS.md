# Charts

These standards apply to every chart, whether it lives here or in the feature that owns its subject. [Decision 0029](../../docs/decisions/0029-the-server-draws-every-chart-as-svg.md) explains why the server draws charts.

- The server draws a chart in SVG as part of its page, so the chart shows before JavaScript loads. What a chart does in the browser, such as a crosshair, a tooltip, or a label placed again, enhances the drawing, and before JavaScript runs the page shows no control that waits for it.
- A chart places its marks, its texts, and the ends of its lines at fractions of its plot, as `PlotFraction`, because only the browser knows the width of the plot. A length that is not a position in the plot is a multiple of the size of the text of the chart, in em, so the chart keeps its proportions when a reader enlarges the text. The texts of the marks of an axis use the font of codes, DM Mono, which decision 0028 sets for them and whose characters are equally wide, so the room for such a text is counted in widths of a character. A name beside a point of a map uses the font of the text, as [decision 0035](../../docs/decisions/0035-the-map-names-its-points-in-words-and-keeps-the-name-of-the-reader.md) sets, so the server places it from an estimate of its width, and the browser places it again from the width that it measures.
- The reader's portfolio and every stock the reader holds keep their name at every width, ahead of every other point. Long account names use the ellipsis of [decision 0047](../../docs/decisions/0047-chart-names-use-an-ellipsis-when-they-are-too-wide.md), while their full text stays outside the plot. Fixed comparison labels stay complete. Only a name's accompanying value may drop when room runs out, and a value never covers the mark of its own point, so it follows its name away from the mark. The server and the browser place these names before the labels of lines, the IHSG, and the stocks that the reader does not hold, which get names only where room remains. A kept name stays apart from other names and nearer its own point before it keeps clear of other marks, and it may cross its own mark and use the room beside the plot that the axis leaves for it, as decision 0035 sets.
- Every fixed label of a map, including starting cash, corners, medians, and reference lines, keeps clear of every point, its selection ring, and every name with its displayed value at every width. It moves to a clear place or is left out when none remains. A fixed label that the references write in full on a desktop and briefly on a phone, such as a corner of the class map, has a text for each width, and the labels of a map take their wide texts together where the measured plot holds every one of them, so a map never mixes the two for want of width. Clearance includes the outline around every edge of the letters. Its offsets use the unit of its placement even when its letters have a smaller font. An estimate checks whether marks or names can cross it as the plot grows, and a measurement belongs only to the data it measured. Browser tests check the rendered labels against names and marks, before and after JavaScript loads, because a calculation alone cannot catch a different unit in the drawing.
- The marks of an axis write their values as the screen writes the same values elsewhere, so an axis of returns since a date, which are gains and losses, gives each mark its sign, such as +6%, and an axis of levels, such as expected monthly returns, does not. An axis holds about as many marks as the references of decision 0028 draw, and a compact map holds fewer. No two marks of an axis read the same. An axis of values marks values no closer than the smallest step that the format of its texts writes apart, which `niceTicks` takes from the format, such as `smallestPercentStep`. An axis of time compares the texts of its dates in the format of its caller, such as a month, and leaves out a mark that repeats the mark before it, as two closes in one month of a sparse history would. Their tests include values that spread less than that step and dates that a format writes alike.
- A chart that a reader explores answers a pointer, a finger, and the keys of the pattern of ARIA that fits it, such as a slider for the dates of a chart over time. Its heading names it, a screen reader reads the marked point as its value, and Escape hides what the chart shows over its drawing.
- The reader's own portfolio takes saffron. A stock's price over time, including a sparkline, takes the gain color when its latest price as shown exceeds its first price as shown, the loss color when it falls, and muted ink when it is unchanged. The price chart of the IHSG takes the color of its change over the range, as the market references draw it. A comparison line, such as the IHSG over time, stays muted. On a map, the IHSG is a diamond in ink, as the references of decision 0028 draw it, because its shape already sets it apart from the dots of the stocks.
