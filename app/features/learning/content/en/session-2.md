# Portfolio theory

## summary

Combined risk depends on asset weights and how their returns move together.

## key-idea

Combining assets whose returns do not move perfectly together can reduce risk relative to the weighted average of their individual risks.

## body

Imagine two companies exposed to different sources of risk. When one has weaker results, the other need not weaken as much. Their combination can be more stable than putting all the money in either one.

This benefit depends on correlation and weights. With weights and standard deviations held fixed, a lower correlation reduces the combined variance. Correlation need not be negative for diversification to help.

Expected return remains the weighted average of the assets' expected returns. Combining assets does not create extra return by itself. Risk below the weighted average need not be below the risk of the most stable asset either.

Adding more stocks alone is not enough. Stocks that depend on the same income sources or economic conditions can move together. Diversification reduces company-specific risk, but market risk remains and losses are still possible.

Correlation from past data is an estimate. The relationship can change, especially when markets are under stress. Consider shared sources of risk as well as the number of names in a portfolio.
