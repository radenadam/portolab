# Options

## summary

An option buyer receives a right, while its seller takes an obligation.

## key-idea

The premium pays for a right to buy or sell at the strike price under the contract's terms.

## body

A call gives its buyer the right to buy the asset, while a put gives the right to sell it. The right applies at the times the contract specifies. The seller must fulfil the contract when the buyer exercises the right.

A buyer can lose the entire premium paid, plus transaction costs. A seller's risk differs. Selling a call without owning its underlying asset can create unlimited losses.

The underlying price, strike price, time remaining, volatility, interest rates, and dividends affect a stock option's value. The right's value at expiry differs from the trade's profit, because premium and costs also count.
