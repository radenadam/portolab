# Product

## Purpose

Portfolio management courses teach how to measure return and risk, why diversification lowers risk, and how models such as CAPM price that risk. Students usually meet these ideas as formulas applied to textbook numbers, so the ideas stay abstract. Portolab lets a class build real portfolios of Indonesian stocks with virtual cash, at real market prices, under the trading rules of the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX). It then computes the measures of the course from each student's own portfolio, so every formula has numbers the student chose. The lecturer uses Portolab to teach in class and to follow every student's work between meetings.

## Users

- **Lecturers** of portfolio management and investment courses at Indonesian universities, starting with one postgraduate management course. A lecturer creates a class, sets its rules, and follows the portfolio of every student.
- **Students** in those courses. Postgraduate management students are often working adults who meet on weekday evenings and Saturdays and do much of their coursework online, on a phone as often as on a computer. Many have never traded, and some already invest through Indonesian apps.

Portolab is not for investors outside a class, for traders who want a trading platform, or for anyone who wants investment advice. It never recommends what to buy or sell.

## Quality bar

- **StockTrak** is the classroom simulator that universities use most. Its lecturers set trade limits, commissions, diversification rules, and start and end dates, and they can rank students by Sharpe ratio, alpha, and beta instead of raw return. It does not cover IDX, has no Indonesian interface, and does not tie portfolio theory to a student's holdings. Portolab matches its class controls for everything Portolab supports, and it is better on all three gaps.
- **Stockbit Virtual Trading** follows IDX mechanics faithfully: trading sessions, lots, tick sizes, auto rejection limits, fees, and the states of an order. It has no class features and no portfolio analytics. Portolab matches its market realism.
- **Portfolio Visualizer** shows risk measures and efficient frontiers clearly, but only for US and Canadian assets. Portolab shows the measures of the course with the same clarity, for IDX stocks, computed from the student's own portfolio.
- **Stockbit, Ajaib, and IPOT** are apps with which many Indonesians trade IDX stocks, and some students already use one of them. Portolab must hold up beside them on a phone, so a stock opens with its price chart, an order shows its costs before the student confirms it, and gains and losses are green and red with a sign and an arrow.
- **Investopedia Simulator** is a free simulator in which a teacher can run a game for a class, as StockTrak runs classes. Portolab must feel as much like trading as both, while it ties every trade to the theory of the course.
- **Duolingo and Khan Academy** teach in short steps, with help where the learner practices, such as the tips of a lesson and the hints of an exercise. Portolab teaches the same way, so practice comes first, and a concept opens where the student acts, with depth on demand.
- **Teaching research** finds that leaderboards by raw return, prizes for rank, and badges push students toward gambling, while trading on fixed dates and judging risk-adjusted return against a benchmark teach portfolio management. Portolab follows the research by design.

[Decision 0028](decisions/0028-every-screen-follows-the-reference-screens-of-peta.md) sets the screens that meet this bar. Beyond these products, Portolab must meet these standards of its own:

- **Every measure can be checked by hand.** A measure shows its formula and its inputs, so a student can reproduce it in a spreadsheet and trust it.
- **Concepts open where students use them.** A "?" beside a measure opens its meaning and the value on the page. Its calculation expands in the card, as decision 0028 sets.
- **Copy teaches.** When the app rejects an action, it states what happened with the real numbers, explains the rule behind it, and offers a next step.
- **Both languages are complete.** Bahasa Indonesia and English cover every screen, message, and number format, and neither is a translation afterthought.

## Scope

### First release

The first release serves the course sessions on CAPM and on portfolio return and risk, and it teaches every other topic of the course in a lesson.

- A lecturer creates a class, and students join it with a code. Accounts need no email.
- A class meets weekly for the 16 sessions of the course, from the date of its first session, which its lecturer gives, as [decision 0032](decisions/0032-a-class-meets-weekly-from-the-date-of-its-first-session.md) explains. The lecturer's Kelas page shows the semester and the session of each week. [Decision 0040](decisions/0040-beranda-keeps-the-class-calendar-in-kelas.md) places the class calendar outside Beranda.
- Each student starts with virtual cash, Rp100 million unless the lecturer sets another amount.
- The order ticket opens beside its stock on a desktop and over it on a phone. It shows the costs before confirmation, and a changed total requires another confirmation, including before JavaScript loads, as [decision 0044](decisions/0044-the-ticket-shows-the-trade-before-confirmation.md) explains.
- The stock page previews a purchase with 5%, 10%, or 20% of the portfolio, within the available cash. The ticket follows the lots and price of the draft. Both show beta, monthly risk, expected monthly return, stock weight, and IDX-IC sector weight before and after the trade, with a small map and the security market line on its beta axis. The comparison names a change in the shared months and explains unavailable measures, as [decision 0031](decisions/0031-sharpe-treynor-and-jensens-alpha-are-measures-of-the-first-release.md) sets.
- Students trade the stocks of the LQ45 index, as decision 0014 explains. They buy only those that have the monthly returns that the measures need, and they can sell a holding even after its stock leaves the index, as decision 0015 explains. Their orders fill at delayed prices quoted in session I and session II of IDX. Decision 0001 describes the prices, and decision 0016 explains the fills. Orders follow IDX lots, tick sizes, auto rejection limits, fees, and the final tax on sales. Limit orders and market orders are supported. An order lasts for one trading day, as decision 0016 explains, and a student can cancel it until it fills or expires. A student may write the reason of an order, which the lecturer of the class reads, as decision 0033 explains. Pasar, the holdings, and the stock page show the name that IDX publishes for the company of each stock, and Pasar and the stock page show its sector of IDX-IC too, as [decision 0034](decisions/0034-a-stock-carries-the-name-and-the-sector-that-idx-publishes.md) explains.
- Beranda at the home of a student's class shows the portfolio value and its daily change, the return over time against the IHSG, and risk against the market. Its map places the portfolio and its holdings among unnamed classmates by beta and return, and its time view marks the sessions from the class calendar. An insight uses the student's portfolio to open a concept from the course in place. The holdings show their weights, costs, prices, gains or losses, and a month of prices ending at each quote, with the cash and what open orders set aside. Prices with the same displayed quote time share one note, and a price with a different time keeps it beside its row. The return counts from when the student could first trade, with final daily closes under decision 0018 and a timestamped current endpoint under [decision 0053](decisions/0053-current-returns-share-one-snapshot.md). The headline, chart endpoint, and map use the same current valuation and IHSG comparison.
- For each stock and for the whole portfolio, Portolab shows the expected return, variance, standard deviation, covariances and correlations, beta against the IHSG, the return that CAPM requires, and the position on the security market line. A stock gets them once it has the monthly returns that decision 0013 requires. Because students buy only such stocks, a portfolio has them too, unless a holding later loses monthly returns, such as after a suspension, or its stocks lack the closes of different months. For the whole portfolio, Portolab also shows the Sharpe ratio, the Treynor ratio, Jensen's alpha, which is the distance from the security market line, and the share of the risk of the portfolio that each stock adds, as [decision 0031](decisions/0031-sharpe-treynor-and-jensens-alpha-are-measures-of-the-first-release.md) explains. A chart places the portfolio and the stocks against the security market line of the months that their measures share, as decision 0023 explains. Long names from accounts use the bounded chart labels of [decision 0047](decisions/0047-chart-names-use-an-ellipsis-when-they-are-too-wide.md).
- The lecturer sees every portfolio and every measure, ranked by a performance measure that the lecturer chooses, as [decision 0041](decisions/0041-the-lecturer-chooses-the-measure-that-ranks-the-class.md) explains, and downloads them for grading as a workbook that every spreadsheet opens, as decision 0022 explains. From Mahasiswa, the lecturer opens a student's read-only Ringkasan, Analisis, and Jurnal dan order at the addresses of decision 0030. These tabs share the student's portfolio, measures, orders, and reasons, and the back link selects the same student with the filters and search kept. Other lecturers and students receive a page that does not exist.
- The lecturer's class overview flags losses, volatility, empty holdings, and students with no order this week. Each flag opens the student or the matching group in Mahasiswa. Its discussion card uses the class data for two questions about the current calendar session, with a link to that session's lesson. Review and exam sessions have no simulator lesson. Its latest reasons name the student, trade, and fill time, and the full list opens in place. The overview places measured portfolios on a map of risk against cumulative return. Its headline compares each student with the IHSG over that student's own period, as [decision 0035](decisions/0035-the-map-names-its-points-in-words-and-keeps-the-name-of-the-reader.md) explains.
- The approved design brings these into the first release besides the reason of an order and the measures of Sharpe, Treynor, and Jensen, as [decision 0028](decisions/0028-every-screen-follows-the-reference-screens-of-peta.md) explains: a lesson for each session of the course that teaches a topic with a glossary, a watchlist, and a demonstration portfolio in which the lecturer trades and which no statistic of the class counts. Its ownership, access, and starting cash follow [decision 0045](decisions/0045-the-lecturer-trades-in-a-demonstration-portfolio.md).
- Lesson and glossary prose ships in both languages as [decision 0036](decisions/0036-lessons-and-the-glossary-are-content-in-both-languages.md) explains. Their sheets and navigation follow [decision 0030](decisions/0030-each-class-is-one-workspace-with-its-own-addresses.md#what-opens-in-place-and-what-navigates). The lessons on CAPM and portfolio risk practise with the student's own data, as [decision 0048](decisions/0048-lesson-practice-keeps-estimates-fixed-while-weights-change.md) explains.
- Pasar lists the current LQ45 stocks inside a class workspace, with search by code or company, sector filters, sorting, and the IHSG chart. A visitor lands on sign-in with a link to join, as [decision 0030](decisions/0030-each-class-is-one-workspace-with-its-own-addresses.md) explains.
- Aktivitas at `/classes/:classId/activity` lists orders with their status, their reason, and cancellation while they are open. Its status filter and the phone's Order or Jurnal choice keep their state in the address and work before JavaScript loads. The journal lists filled trades, their reasons, the price change since each trade, and the net realised gain of a sale from the average purchase cost. It names an unavailable comparison when no quote is as recent as the fill, and an unavailable sale gain when deleted purchase records leave its cost unknown.
- Every screen works on a phone, a tablet, and a computer.
- Every result is labelled as a simulation, and the price delay is always visible.
- A sample command creates a synthetic class in a separate database for exploration. Its frame labels the sample on every page, as [decision 0050](decisions/0050-a-sample-class-lives-in-a-database-of-its-own.md) explains.
- Lecturers open a guide in Bahasa Indonesia or English from their profile menu. It explains the semester workflow and simulation limits, and ships with the app for reading on a home network, as [decision 0052](decisions/0052-the-guide-for-lecturers-lives-in-the-repository-and-in-the-app.md) explains.

### Later releases

Each later release lets students practice the next course topic in the simulation, in teaching order: futures and hedging, mutual funds, options, and bonds. The first release teaches these topics only in their lessons. After those come the efficient frontier and portfolios managed by teams.

### Non-goals

- **Real money.** Portolab never connects to a brokerage account and never places a real order.
- **Advice.** Portolab never recommends securities, because recommendations are regulated in Indonesia and because choosing is what students must learn.
- **Rewards for rank.** No leaderboard by raw return, no prizes, no badges, and no streaks, because they teach gambling.
- **Real-time prices.** Real-time IDX data requires a license that costs far more than the project can pay. Delayed prices are enough to teach the course.
- **Other markets.** Crypto, foreign exchange, and foreign stocks are outside the syllabus.

## Constraints

- **Platforms**: current browsers on phones, tablets, and computers, from a screen 360 pixels wide.
- **Client code**: each route and the JavaScript shared by every route have budgets, as [decision 0038](decisions/0038-client-javascript-is-budgeted-by-route.md) explains. Each route's CSS has a budget too, as [decision 0042](decisions/0042-client-css-is-budgeted-by-route.md) explains.
- **Hosting**: one Linux VPS that the lecturer owns. Its owner deploys and updates Portolab from the public repository by following [the deployment guide](deploy.md), so the guide must work step by step on a fresh server, and also on a server that already runs other work, which the guide leaves as it is. Demonstrations also run on a laptop and serve phones on the same Wi-Fi network. The isolated sample class also runs in Docker on WSL over HTTP on the home network, and on the VPS over HTTPS at its own subdomain, as [decision 0050](decisions/0050-a-sample-class-lives-in-a-database-of-its-own.md) requires. The project uses no paid service.
- **Market data**: delayed prices come from Yahoo Finance, which has no license for this use and limits automated requests. The source sits behind one interface, every price is cached, and the app keeps working with the last known prices when the source fails. A licensed source can replace it without changes elsewhere.
- **Company logos**: the repository carries monogram tiles only. The server owner may supply raster logos from a folder outside it, as [decision 0056](decisions/0056-the-owner-supplies-company-logos-outside-the-repository.md) sets.
- **Privacy**: Indonesia's personal data protection law applies. Portolab stores only what a class needs, asks for explicit consent, and holds no real financial data.
- **Regulation**: OJK rules on financial information allow simulations that are labelled as simulations and that make no recommendations.
- **Schedule**: the first release reaches the students of the first class in the remaining sessions of the semester, which ends around 21 December 2026, as issue #127 tracks.
